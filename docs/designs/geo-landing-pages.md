## Problem Statement

VolunteerReady has zero signups. The founder lives in California's Central Valley and wants to create geo-targeted landing pages to acquire nonprofit organizations in 5 target areas: San Joaquin County, Stanislaus County, Modesto, Stockton, and Sacramento. The hypothesis is that local targeting converts better than broad national marketing, especially for small nonprofits currently managing volunteers through spreadsheets and email.

## Demand Evidence

Hypothesis only. No direct conversations with local nonprofits. No organic search traffic patterns to analyze. The founder's proximity to the Central Valley nonprofit ecosystem is the primary signal. This is a pre-validation bet.

Key landscape signal: VolunteerMatch and Idealist own volunteer-facing geo pages. Nobody has nonprofit-admin-facing geo pages for volunteer screening/management. That gap is real.

## Status Quo

Small Central Valley nonprofits manage volunteers through:
- Gmail inboxes for applications
- Google Sheets for tracking
- No formal screening unless a grant requires it
- Background checks done ad-hoc when mandated, often scrambled together last-minute
- No credential tracking, no audit trails

The cost: staff time wasted on coordination, compliance risk, funder documentation compiled from sign-in sheets the week before reports are due.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target user:** Nonprofit program director or executive director at a small-to-mid-size Central Valley nonprofit (10-200 volunteers). They have a grant that requires volunteer background checks, or an insurer that mandates screening. They're currently doing this manually and it's painful.

**Narrowest wedge:** 5 location-specific landing pages targeting nonprofit admins searching for volunteer screening, background checks, and compliance tools. Each page leads with operational pain (not geography), uses location as a trust signal, and captures leads with source attribution. (Update 2026-07-13: expanded to 6 locations post-launch with the addition of Fresno — see `src/lib/locations.ts`.)

## Constraints

- Must integrate with existing public-pages.ts registry, sitemap.ts, and OG image system
- Must follow existing component patterns (PublicHero, FaqSection, CTABanner, etc.)
- Must follow DESIGN.md visual system (Porto warm palette)
- Content must be genuinely unique per location (not template city-name swaps)
- Architecture: app/ = routing only, no Prisma in UI, services layer for business logic

## Premises

1. **Pages lead with operational pain, geography is secondary.** (Revised per Codex challenge.) A page that says "FCRA-compliant background checks for your volunteer program" is stronger than one that says "Serving Modesto nonprofits." Geography is a trust signal and SEO hook, not the headline value prop.
2. **Primary target = nonprofit admins, not volunteers.** Volunteer-facing pages compete with VolunteerMatch (massive domain authority). Nonprofit admin pages targeting "volunteer screening [city]" have near-zero competition.
3. **Local specificity builds trust as a secondary signal.** Mentioning county-specific nonprofit counts, named local organizations, and area-specific pain points signals "this company knows our community."
4. **Content must be genuinely unique per location.** Google penalizes programmatic pages that are just city-name swaps. Each page needs real local data and genuinely different copy.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Codex independently validated the nonprofit-admin-only targeting and the "sales asset, not SEO play" framing. Key challenge: Codex argued that problem-specific proof beats location-specific copy for credibility. Evidence: outreach gets better reply/demo rates from pages framed around operational pain than from pages framed around geography. This led to revising premise #1.

Codex's 48-hour prototype: 4 landing pages with unique local copy, workflow diagrams, sample screening checklists, lead capture with source tagging, and event tracking. Skip volunteer-facing pages, programmatic SEO at scale, AI-generated content, and full self-serve signup.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Static Data-Driven Pages (Minimal)
Dynamic route at `src/app/(public)/locations/[slug]/page.tsx` with a data file containing per-region content. Uses `generateStaticParams` for ISR. Reuses existing components. No lead capture.
- Effort: S | Risk: Low | Completeness: 5/10
- Pro: Smallest diff, fastest to ship
- Con: No conversion data, no way to know what works

### Approach B: Full Local Pages with Lead Capture (Selected)
Everything in A, plus:
- Lead capture form (org name, contact email, volunteer count, current process, pain points) writing to new LeadCapture Prisma table
- Source-tagged by location slug for conversion attribution
- Event tracking for page-level funnel analytics
- Genuinely unique local content per area: county nonprofit counts, named local orgs, area-specific pain points, sample screening workflow diagram, sample compliance checklist
- Effort: M | Risk: Low-Med | Completeness: 9/10

### Approach C: Outreach-First Landing Pages (Creative)
Pages designed as cold-email collateral: comparison calculator, sample screening checklist per county, single CTA. Less SEO-optimized but directly addresses the zero-signups problem.
- Effort: M | Risk: Low | Completeness: 7/10

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B: Full Local Pages with Lead Capture.**

Rationale: B gives lead capture with source attribution, which is the single most important thing for a product with zero signups. You need to know which locations generate interest, not just traffic.

  metaDescription: string;         // SEO description

**Page structure per location (7 sections, conversion-first):**
1. Hero: 7/5 asymmetric layout. Left: operational pain headline in Fraunces 700 48px + geography secondary eyebrow in Geist uppercase 12px + one-sentence description in Geist 18px + two CTAs (filled "Get Set Up Free" + outline "See How It Works"). Right: real VolunteerReady dashboard screenshot (reuse ScreenshotSection pattern, annotated with 2-3 local callouts). Left-aligned, not centered.
2. Pain acknowledgment: `bg-muted` section, `max-w-2xl`. Fraunces 24px heading "Sound familiar?". Bullet list with `text-warning` checkmarks showing location-specific pain points. Conversational Geist body text.
3. Lead capture form: cream background with white Card (12px radius). Fraunces 32px heading "Ready to simplify your volunteer program?". Full form: org name, email, volunteer count, current process, pain points. Forest green submit button. Privacy notice. (See interaction states spec below.)
4. Comparison table: `bg-muted` section, `max-w-3xl mx-auto`. Fraunces 32px heading "What changes". Two-column layout using a real `<table>` (not cards): left column header "Without VolunteerReady" in `text-muted-foreground`, right column header "With VolunteerReady" in `text-primary`. Each row: left cell has X icon (`text-destructive`, 16px) + manual process pain point; right cell has check icon (`text-success`, 16px) + automated solution. 4-6 rows per location, drawn from `comparisonItems` in location data. Table cells use Geist 14px. Subtle `border-border/40` row dividers. No colored backgrounds on cells, no icon circles, no card wrappers.
5. Local proof: cream background. Large Fraunces number (48px, `text-primary`) for nonprofit count + Geist subtext. Named local orgs in prose (not a bulleted list). PlatformStatsBar below. Section is conditionally rendered only when `localProof` data exists in location config.
6. FAQ: accordion style (reuse FaqSection), first item open. Location-specific questions.
7. CTA banner: full-width `bg-primary` background. White Fraunces heading + white button with green text. Chains to booking link. After form submission success, this CTA becomes the primary next step.

**Dark mode constraint:** All new components (LocationHero, lead-capture-form, comparison table, local proof section) MUST use semantic Tailwind tokens only (text-primary, bg-muted, text-foreground, bg-background, text-destructive, text-success, etc.). No hardcoded hex values anywhere in component code. Dark mode is handled automatically by the design system's CSS variable layer. This constraint prevents the dark-mode-hue-mismatch pitfall found in prior work.

**Accessibility requirements:**
- Lead form: `<label htmlFor>` on all fields, `aria-required="true"` on org name + email, `aria-describedby` linking error messages to fields, focus management (focus first invalid field on validation failure)
- FAQ: existing `<details>`/`<summary>` is keyboard-accessible by default
- CTA buttons: 44px minimum touch targets (met by `size="lg"`)
- Comparison table: `<th scope="col">` on column headers, semantic `<table>` markup
- Color contrast: all text/background combinations meet WCAG 2.1 AA (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text) — verified by design system tokens
- Skip link: include in page layout for keyboard users to skip to lead form
- Form success state: `role="status"` + `aria-live="polite"` so screen readers announce the confirmation

**Motion spec (3 intentional motions, per DESIGN.md minimal-functional):**
1. Hero screenshot: `FadeInOnScroll` with 200ms delay (screenshot appears as user's eye moves right)
2. Comparison table rows: staggered `FadeInOnScroll` with 75ms delay per row (reuse existing pattern from FaqSection)
3. Lead form success state: 250ms ease-out transition from form to confirmation card (opacity + translateY)
No other animations. No decorative motion. These three serve comprehension.

**Lead form analytics events (via consented-analytics gtag pattern):**
- `lead_form_view` — fires when form section scrolls into viewport (IntersectionObserver), params: `{ location_slug }`
- `lead_form_start` — fires on first field focus, params: `{ location_slug }`
- `lead_form_submit` — fires on submit click, params: `{ location_slug }`
- `lead_form_success` — fires on successful mutation, params: `{ location_slug }`
- `lead_form_error` — fires on mutation error, params: `{ location_slug, error_type }`
- `lead_booking_click` — fires when user clicks "book a call" CTA in success state, params: `{ location_slug }`
All events respect consent gating via the existing cookie banner pattern.

**Spam prevention:** Honeypot hidden input with `name="website"`, visually hidden via `sr-only` + `aria-hidden="true"` + `tabIndex={-1}`. If field has any value on submit, silently reject: return success response to the client but do not save to DB or send notifications. No error message to the bot. Rate limiting on tRPC mutation (5 per IP per hour). No reCAPTCHA needed at this scale.

**Lead notification:** On new lead, send email to founder (via existing `sendEmail` utility) with lead details and source location. No admin UI needed initially; leads are also queryable via Prisma Studio or a future admin view.

**Duplicate handling:** If same contactEmail + locationSlug already exists, update the existing record rather than creating a duplicate.

**Example FAQ questions per location (founder should refine, but this sets the bar for "localized"):**
- **Stockton:** "Do agricultural nonprofits in San Joaquin County need volunteer background checks?" / "How does VolunteerReady work with seasonal volunteer programs?" / "Can VolunteerReady help with port district compliance requirements?"
- **Modesto:** "How do Stanislaus County nonprofits handle volunteer screening for after-school programs?" / "Is VolunteerReady compatible with existing background check providers in Modesto?"
- **San Joaquin County:** "How many nonprofits in San Joaquin County are required to screen volunteers?" / "What grant compliance requirements apply to SJC volunteer programs?"
- **Stanislaus County:** "Do youth-serving nonprofits in Stanislaus County need FCRA-compliant background checks?" / "How does VolunteerReady reduce volunteer coordinator burnout?"
- **Sacramento:** "How do state-funded Sacramento nonprofits document volunteer compliance?" / "Can larger Sacramento volunteer programs integrate VolunteerReady with existing HR systems?"

Generic FAQ questions that just swap city names (e.g., "How does VolunteerReady help nonprofits in [City]?") are NOT acceptable. Each question must reference a real local concern.
