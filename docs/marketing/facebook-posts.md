## SaaS Founder Groups (SaaS Growth Hacks / SaaS Founders / Scaling SaaS Founders)

These groups usually have a post format — check if they use [ASK]/[GIVE]
tags or similar before posting. Adapted below for that style.

**Post:**

[ASK] Selling SaaS to nonprofits — anyone done it?

I'm a solo dev building volunteer management software for nonprofits
(screening, background checks, shift scheduling, portable credentials).
Product is live, zero paying users so far.

The thing I keep running into: my buyer isn't evaluating me against
competitors. They're evaluating me against the spreadsheet they've been
using for five years. And their budget for software is often literally $0.

I've been doing discovery conversations on Reddit and the feedback is
useful but it's all from end users (volunteer coordinators, social
workers). I haven't cracked the "who actually pays for this" question yet.

A few specific things I'd love input on:

- Has anyone sold into nonprofits successfully? What was the sales motion?
- My pricing is Free / $49 / $149. Too cheap? Too expensive? Wrong tiers?
- Is founder-led onboarding the right move at this stage, or should I be
  pushing for self-serve?

Site is volunteerready.org if you want context. Roast the landing page
while you're at it.

## Volunteer Coordinator Resource Community (Track It Forward group)

NOTE: This group bans product promotion. This post is a pure discussion
starter. Do NOT mention VolunteerReady or link to the site. Only bring it
up if someone asks what you're working on, and even then keep it brief.

**Post:**

Question for the group — how do you handle background check renewals?

I've been having conversations with coordinators at different orgs and
I'm hearing two very different approaches:

1. One and done — run the check when they start, never revisit it
2. Batch renewals — pick a month (one person told me January) and re-run
   everyone at the same time regardless of when they started

For the orgs that do renewals: how are you tracking when someone's due?
Is it a spreadsheet, a calendar reminder, or something more structured?

And for the "one and done" folks: is that a policy decision, or is it
more that tracking renewals is too much work without a system for it?

Trying to understand whether this is a pain point people actively think
about or more of a "we know we should but we don't" situation.

## Local Nonprofit / Utah Groups

Search for: "Utah Nonprofits", "[your city] Nonprofit Network",
"Salt Lake Nonprofit Professionals", or similar local groups.

**Post:**

Anyone here manage volunteers at a local nonprofit?

I'm a developer based in Utah and I've been researching how nonprofits
handle volunteer screening and onboarding. I've been volunteering locally
and talking to coordinators — the range is wild. Some orgs run full
background checks and multi-step interviews. Others hand you a name tag
and point you toward the work.

I'm not selling anything — I'm trying to understand what the process
actually looks like here in Utah, especially since a lot of it seems to
run through DPS. A few things I'm curious about:

- If you use volunteers, how much of the onboarding process is manual?
- Do you run background checks? If so, through what provider?
- What's the biggest time sink in your volunteer program?

I'm building tools in this space and I want to make sure I'm solving
problems that actually exist locally, not just problems I imagine from
behind a screen. Happy to grab coffee with anyone who's willing to talk
about this stuff.

## AL!VE (Association of Leaders in Volunteer Engagement)

Check if they have a Facebook group or active page with discussion.
This audience is professional volunteer managers — they take their work
seriously and will engage with substantive questions.

**Post:**

Credential portability across organizations — is this something the
field is thinking about?

I've been doing research on volunteer management and one pattern I keep
seeing: a volunteer who's been background-checked, trained, and verified
at one organization has to start completely from scratch when they sign
up at a different org. Same checks, same trainings, no transfer.

From a volunteer's perspective, this seems like unnecessary friction.
But from an org's perspective, I can see why you'd want to run your own
checks — liability, different standards, different populations served.

For people who've been in volunteer engagement for a while:

- Is credential portability something that's been discussed in the field?
- Are there any standards or frameworks for recognizing screening done
  at another organization?
- Or is the "start fresh every time" approach the right one for liability
  reasons?

Genuinely curious about the professional perspective on this. I'm a
developer exploring this space and I want to understand the nuance
before assuming technology can solve what might be a policy problem.
