# Nonprofit Discovery Outreach — Conversation Flow

This is a practical script for reaching out to nonprofit volunteer coordinators
and running discovery conversations. The goal is **conversations, not signups**.
You're trying to learn what their real pain is, in their own words.

---

## Channel 1: Cold Outreach Email

### Initial Email

**Subject:** Learning from volunteer coordinators — 30 minutes?

**Body:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm Jason, a software developer working on tools for nonprofit volunteer
> management. I'm not selling anything — I'm in the research phase and trying
> to understand how volunteer coordinators actually work day-to-day.
>
> I found [Org Name] through [Charity Navigator / GuideStar / your website]
> and was impressed by [specific thing — their volunteer program, a recent
> event, their mission]. I'd love to hear about your experience managing
> volunteers — what works, what's frustrating, what you wish existed.
>
> Would you be open to a 30-minute call or coffee? I'm happy to work around
> your schedule. No pitch, just listening.
>
> Thanks,
> Jason

**Personalization checklist:**
- [ ] Use their actual name (not "Volunteer Coordinator")
- [ ] Reference something specific about their org (mission, a recent event, size)
- [ ] Mention HOW you found them (adds credibility, not random spam)

### Follow-up (5 business days later, if no response)

**Subject:** Re: Learning from volunteer coordinators — 30 minutes?

> Hi [Name],
>
> Just bumping this — I know volunteer coordinators are busy (that's part of
> what I'm trying to understand!). If a call doesn't work, I'd also be happy
> to send 3-4 quick questions over email instead.
>
> Either way, no pressure. Thanks for what you do at [Org Name].
>
> Jason

### Final follow-up (5 more business days)

**Subject:** Re: Learning from volunteer coordinators — 30 minutes?

> Hi [Name],
>
> Last note from me — I don't want to be a pest. If the timing isn't right,
> totally understand. If you know another volunteer coordinator who might be
> open to chatting, I'd appreciate a name.
>
> Thanks,
> Jason

**Stop after 3 touches.** Never send a 4th email. Expect ~5-10% reply rate.

## Channel 2: LinkedIn / Community Warm Outreach

Use after 1-2 weeks of participating in groups (answering questions, sharing
relevant articles, commenting on others' posts).

### LinkedIn Connection Request

> Hi [Name], I've been following the [Group Name] discussions and your posts
> about [specific topic they discussed]. I'm a developer researching how
> volunteer coordinators manage their workflows — would love to connect.

### After They Accept

> Thanks for connecting! I'm building software for volunteer management and
> I'm in the learning phase — talking to coordinators to understand what
> actually matters vs. what tech people assume matters.
>
> Would you be open to a 30-minute call? I'm genuinely just listening — no
> demo, no pitch. Happy to buy you coffee if you're in [city].

---

## Channel 3: Volunteering Relationship

After you've volunteered at an org for 2+ shifts and have a natural
relationship with the coordinator.

### The Ask (in person or via email after a shift)

> Hey [Name], I've really enjoyed volunteering here. I actually have a
> background in software development and I've been curious about the
> behind-the-scenes of managing volunteers.
>
> I noticed [specific observation from volunteering — e.g., "you were
> juggling a lot of texts during shift changes" or "the sign-in process
> seemed pretty manual"]. Would you be open to telling me more about what
> that's like from your side? I'd love to buy you coffee sometime.

This channel has the highest conversion rate because you've already
built trust through showing up and doing the work.

## The Discovery Conversation (30 minutes)

### Opening (2 minutes)

> Thanks so much for making time. Quick context: I'm a software developer
> and I'm in the early stages of building tools for volunteer management.
> I'm NOT here to pitch anything — I genuinely want to understand your
> world. Everything you tell me helps me build something that actually
> matters instead of what I *think* matters.
>
> There are no wrong answers. I'm going to ask about your day-to-day and
> what's hard. Ready?

**Rules for yourself:**
- Listen more than you talk (aim for 80/20)
- Don't describe your product unless they ask
- Don't offer solutions — just understand problems
- Take notes (ask permission: "Mind if I jot down notes?")
- Record verbatim quotes when they describe pain

### Question 1: The Workflow (5 minutes)

> Walk me through what happens when a new volunteer signs up at [Org Name].
> From the moment they express interest to their first shift — what does
> that process look like?

**Listen for:** Steps, tools, handoffs, delays, manual work, things that
fall through the cracks.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "What's the typical timeline from sign-up to first shift?"
- "Where does that process break down most often?"
- "What happens when someone falls off — do you follow up?"

### Question 2: The Pain (5 minutes)

> What's the most annoying part of your week related to managing volunteers?

**Listen for:** Specific frustrations, time sinks, things that make them
sigh or laugh ruefully. The more emotional the response, the more real
the pain.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "Tell me more about that."
- "What happened the last time that broke down?"
- "How much time does that eat up in a typical week?"
- "If that problem vanished tomorrow, what would you do with that time?"

### Question 3: Screening & Compliance (5 minutes)

> How do you handle background checks and screening for new volunteers?

**Listen for:** Whether this is a big deal or an afterthought. Different
org types will care differently. Youth-serving and healthcare orgs will
have more to say.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "How long does the background check process take end-to-end?"
- "What happens when someone's check comes back with something?"
- "Do you track credential expirations? How?"
- "Has a screening gap ever caused a problem?"

### Question 4: The Magic Wand (5 minutes)

> If you could wave a magic wand and change one thing about managing
> volunteers, what would it be?

**Listen for:** Their #1 wish. This often reveals the REAL priority,
which may be different from what they complained about earlier. The
complaint is the symptom; the wish is the cure they imagine.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "Why that specifically?"
- "Have you tried to solve that? What happened?"
- "Would your board/director care about that, or is it just your pain?"

### Question 5: Tools & Software (5 minutes)

> What software or tools do you use today to manage volunteers? What do
> you wish it did that it doesn't?

**Listen for:** Specific tool names, what they like/hate, what they've
tried and abandoned, budget constraints, decision-making process.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "How did you end up with [tool]? Who chose it?"
- "What would make you switch to something else?"
- "What's your budget for volunteer management tools?"
- "Who makes software decisions at your org?"

### Closing (3 minutes)

> This has been incredibly helpful. Two last questions:
>
> 1. Is there anything I didn't ask about that I should have?
> 2. Do you know any other volunteer coordinators who might be open to a
>    similar conversation? I'm trying to talk to 10 people total.

**The referral ask is critical.** Warm introductions convert at 3-5x the
rate of cold outreach. Even one referral per conversation compounds fast.

**After the call:**
- Send a thank-you email within 24 hours
- Log the conversation immediately (see template below)
- If they referred someone, reach out within 48 hours while the intro is warm

### Thank-You Email

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks again for taking the time to talk today. Your perspective on
> [specific thing they said] was really eye-opening — I hadn't thought
> about it that way.
>
> I'll keep you posted on what I'm building. If you ever want to see an
> early version down the road, you'll be first on my list.
>
> Thanks,
> Jason

## If They Ask About Your Product

They will sometimes ask "so what are you building?" Here's how to handle it:

**Before you have patterns (conversations 1-5):**

> I'm still figuring that out, honestly. I know I want to help volunteer
> coordinators with [general area], but I want to make sure I'm solving the
> right problem before I build the wrong thing. That's why conversations
> like this are so valuable.

**After you have patterns (conversations 6+):**

> Based on what I've been hearing from coordinators, the biggest pain seems
> to be [pattern you've identified]. I'm building a tool that [brief
> description]. Does that resonate with what you experience?

If they seem interested: "Would you want to be one of the first people to
try it when it's ready? I'd love your feedback."

**Never demo during a discovery call.** The moment you demo, the
conversation shifts from their problems to your solution. Save demos for
after you've validated the pain.

## Pattern Recognition (after 5+ conversations)

After every 5 conversations, answer these questions:

1. **What pain came up most?** List the top 3 complaints, with how many
   people mentioned each.

2. **What words do they use?** Write down the exact phrases coordinators
   use to describe their problems. These become your marketing copy.

3. **Which org type hurts most?** Youth-serving? Food banks? Shelters?
   The org type with the most acute pain is your beachhead market.

4. **Is screening the wedge?** Or is it scheduling, retention, communication,
   or something else? Let the data tell you.

5. **Who's the buyer?** Is it the coordinator, the executive director, the
   board? This determines your sales motion.

6. **What's the budget reality?** Can they pay? How much? Monthly or annual?
   Grant-funded or operational budget?

## Finding Nonprofits to Contact

**Online directories:**
- Charity Navigator (charitynavigator.org) — search by city + category
- GuideStar / Candid (guidestar.org) — detailed nonprofit profiles
- Idealist (idealist.org) — filter for orgs with volunteer programs

**Local sources:**
- Your city's Volunteer Center or United Way chapter
- Local Association of Volunteer Administrators (AVA)
- Community foundation websites
- Local newspaper coverage of nonprofit events

**LinkedIn search:**
- Search "volunteer coordinator" + [your city]
- Search "volunteer manager" + [your city]
- Search "director of volunteers" + [your city]
- Filter by nonprofit industry

**Target mix for your first 10 conversations:**
- 3 high-volume orgs (food banks, shelters, Habitat chapters)
- 3 compliance-heavy orgs (youth-serving, healthcare, schools)
- 2 small community orgs (< 50 volunteers, 1-2 staff)
- 2 referrals from earlier conversations
