# Cold Outreach Emails — Product Stage

These are for now that you have a live product. Your original discovery
emails (outreach-conversation-flow.md) still work for pure research
conversations, but these are more honest about where you're at.

The goal is still **conversations and feedback, not signups**. But you're
no longer pretending you don't have a product.

---

## Initial Email

**Subject:** Volunteer management — built something, want your honest take

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm Jason, a software developer. I've spent the last several months
> volunteering at local orgs and talking to volunteer coordinators about
> what their day-to-day looks like. The same things kept coming up:
> screening is manual, background checks are a time sink, and nothing a
> volunteer does at one org carries over to the next.
>
> So I built something. It's called VolunteerReady — it handles screening,
> background checks, shift scheduling, and portable credentials so a
> volunteer's verified history follows them between organizations.
>
> I found [Org Name] through [source] and [specific reason you noticed
> them — their volunteer program size, a recent event, their mission].
> I'd love to get your honest reaction — not as a sales pitch, but because
> I'd rather hear what's wrong with it from someone who actually does this
> work than guess from my desk.
>
> Would you be open to 20 minutes? Happy to work around your schedule.
> And if you just want to look first, it's at volunteerready.org.
>
> Thanks,
> Jason

**Personalization checklist:**
- [ ] Their actual name
- [ ] How you found them
- [ ] Something specific about their org (not generic flattery)
- [ ] Keep it under 150 words — coordinators are busy

**What's different from the discovery version:** You're upfront that you
built something. You're asking for their reaction to it, not asking them
to teach you about their world. The ask is smaller (20 min, not 30) because
you're more focused now.

## Follow-up (5 business days later)

**Subject:** Re: Volunteer management — built something, want your honest take

> Hi [Name],
>
> Just following up — I know you're busy. If a call doesn't work, I'd
> honestly value even a 2-minute email reply to one question:
>
> What's the most time-consuming part of onboarding a new volunteer at
> [Org Name]?
>
> That alone would help me figure out if I'm solving the right problem.
>
> Either way, thanks for what you do.
>
> Jason

**Why this works:** Asking one specific question is easier to reply to
than agreeing to a call. And their answer tells you whether your product
hits their actual pain point.

## Final follow-up (5 more business days)

**Subject:** Re: Volunteer management — built something, want your honest take

> Hi [Name],
>
> Last note from me. If the timing's off, totally get it. If you happen
> to know another volunteer coordinator who might have 20 minutes for a
> conversation like this, I'd appreciate a name.

**Same rule: stop after 3 touches.**

---

## When to use which email set

**Use the original discovery emails when:**
- You're reaching out to learn about a specific org type you don't understand yet
- You want to validate whether a problem exists before showing your solution
- The contact is at a large or institutional org where "some developer with a product" will get ignored

**Use these product-stage emails when:**
- You want feedback on the actual product from someone in the space
- The contact is at a small-to-mid org where a solo founder story resonates
- You've already validated the problem and want to test whether your solution lands
